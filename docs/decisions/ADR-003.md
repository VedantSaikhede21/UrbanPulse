# ADR-003: Why Nine, Trust, Today's City — Sprint 1C Narrative Decisions

**Status:** Accepted  
**Date:** 2026-07-24  
**Context:** Sprint 1C — completing the landing page narrative arc with three remaining sections

---

## Why Nine Specialists?

**Problem:** The architecture uses nine specialized AI agents, but visitors saw a 5-step pipeline timeline and had no understanding of *why* multiple agents exist instead of one generic model.

**Previous approach (rejected):** Agent cards with technical labels (Vision Agent, Fraud Agent, Priority Agent, Routing Agent) — only AI developers understood them.

**Alternative considered (Concept 2 — The Municipal Team):** Analogy comparing agents to city department roles (Front Desk, Inspector, Supervisor, Dispatch, QA). Rejected because the analogy felt stretched and risked confusing the audience with two parallel models.

**Alternative considered (Concept 3 — The Error Chain):** Defense-in-depth flow showing how errors are caught at each stage. Rejected because it felt defensive and was visually complex.

**Chosen approach (Concept 1 — Story Flow):** A horizontal miniature story: Citizen uploads photo → Vision "I found a pothole" → Priority "This affects a school road" → Routing "Roads Department" → Verification "Repair confirmed." Each specialist speaks in a quote. Connected by arrow icons. No cards listing features — every step is a narrative beat.

**Evidence:** ChatGPT review confirmed static cards were better than technical agent labels but still felt like a feature grid. The story-flow approach makes the architecture intuitively understandable without explanation.

---

## Trust Section

**Problem:** Municipal stakeholders need to justify adoption to supervisors. The landing page had no trust signals beyond implied reliability.

**Alternative considered:** Security badges, compliance logos, testimonial carousel. Rejected — violates ANTI_PATTERNS.md (testimonial carousel), and badges feel inauthentic without actual compliance certifications.

**Chosen approach:** Four trust pillars (Explainable, Transparent, Auditable, Accountable) grounded in product reality — "If an officer overrides a recommendation, that decision is preserved" not "We value transparency." Plus a closing statement: "Every recommendation can be challenged. Every override is recorded. Every decision can be audited."

**Position in flow:** Placed after Today's City so evidence of real outcomes comes before the trust argument.

---

## Today's City Rewrite

**Problem:** Previous section answered "Which ward has the highest score?" — nobody asks that. The Urban Health Score was a computed abstraction that explained itself instead of showing outcomes.

**Removed:** Ward score bars (Ward A—Downtown: 88, Ward B—Industrial: 72, etc.), "Analytics Agent continuously computes" explanation, Urban Health Score numerical display.

**Chosen approach:** Statistics that answer "What changed across the city today?" — 412 reported, 389 resolved (94%), avg response 2h 14m, avg repair 47m. Plus live city narrative: "Fastest department: Roads" and "Most improved: Ward 12." Labeled "Example data · demo" to avoid implying live data.

---

## Consequences

- The landing page now has a complete documentary arc: problem → mechanism → evidence → trust → action
- All seven sections answer exactly one user question each
- No section uses technical language, agent architecture exposition, or analytics dashboard patterns
- The landing page is frozen at v1.0 RC1 (Product Review ≥ 9.0/10) — no further redesigns unless P0 usability issue
