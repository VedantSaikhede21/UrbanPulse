# Product North Star

## North Star Metric

**First-time visitor understands UrbanPulse in under 10 seconds.**

A visitor who lands on urbanpulse.ai should know:
- What this product does
- Why to trust it
- What to do next

Every feature, every sprint, every design decision must contribute to this.

---

## Sprint Objectives

Every sprint answers one question:

> **If we could only improve one thing this sprint, what would it be?**

This is the sprint's **single objective**. Everything else is secondary.

### Good sprint objectives

| Objective | Why it works |
|-----------|-------------|
| "Make the landing page unforgettable" | Emotional, measurable, directional |
| "Reduce citizen report submission time by 30%" | Quantitative, user-impacting |
| "Make the AI pipeline feel alive" | Observable, quality-focused |
| "Prove trust in under 3 seconds" | Specific, testable |

### Weak sprint objectives

| Objective | Why it fails |
|-----------|-------------|
| "Add animations" | No purpose, no measurement |
| "Refactor the codebase" | Internal, not user-facing |
| "Make it look better" | Subjective, no criteria |

---

## Success Criteria

Every sprint defines success criteria before implementation begins:

| Criterion | Question |
|-----------|----------|
| Understandable | Can a first-time visitor explain UrbanPulse after viewing? |
| Trustworthy | Does the page feel government-grade, not gimmicky? |
| Desirable | Would the visitor want to try it? |
| Memorable | Would someone remember this tomorrow? |

If the sprint's output doesn't move these criteria forward, it doesn't ship.

---

## Guardrails

- The North Star is the **only** metric that matters for product direction.
- Technical metrics (bundle size, LCP, INP) are enabling metrics, not North Star metrics.
- A sprint can optimize both, but North Star gets priority.
- The Creative Director has veto power on anything that doesn't serve the North Star.
