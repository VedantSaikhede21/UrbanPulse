# Landing Page Narrative Research — Sprint 1B

## Sources Analyzed

### Apple iPhone Page
- Product as enabler of human experience, not tech spec list
- "Shot on iPhone" campaign — show what people create, not what the phone does
- Narrative structure: problem → desire → reveal → emotional resolution
- Key insight: Apple never leads with features in storytelling sections. They lead with the *moment* the product enables.

### Stripe Narrative Arc
- Restraint, authenticity, intentionality in every word
- Best Stripe pages sell the outcome, not the product
- Lead with the transformation ("Millions of companies..." not "Our payment platform...")
- Subheads answer "why should I care"

### Linear Product Journey
- Product interface dominates hero — show real work happening
- Speed as primary selling point communicated through UI, not copy
- Category clarity in first 3-4 words ("Linear is a purpose-built..." — tells you exactly what it is)

### Story-Driven Landing Pattern (The Narrative LP Method)
1. **Set the scene** — Establish the world the customer lives in today
2. **Build desire** — Show what could be different
3. **Reveal the hero** — The product as the enabler
4. **Make it personal** — User can see themselves in the story
5. **Light the CTA** — The next logical step in the narrative

### Core Architecture of a Story-Driven Landing Page
- **Hero** → Who it's for, what they can achieve
- **Conflict** → What's wrong with today (the problem)
- **Guide** → How the product resolves the conflict
- **Resolution** → What life looks like after
- **CTA** → The natural next step

## Key Principles for UrbanPulse

1. **Information → Emotion**: Every section's primary goal is emotional, not informational
2. **Before/After contrast**: "Today vs UrbanPulse" is more powerful than feature lists
3. **Specificity beats abstraction**: "A neighborhood in Jakarta" > "a city"; "tree falls on power line" > "infrastructure issue"
4. **Show the cycle, don't describe it**: The citizen→photo→AI→officer→repair→notification loop should be experienced, not explained
5. **One question per section**: If a section answers multiple questions, split it. If it answers none, remove it.
6. **Tension before release**: Create a "this is broken" feeling before revealing the solution
7. **Trust through transparency**: Show how the system works (not just that it works)

## Patterns to Avoid

- Fear-based urgency colonizing developing cities
- Talking down to city officials
- Dark dystopian framing (broken cities)
- Feature-dump without context
- Generic "smart city" claims
- Vague terms like "leverage," "optimize," "streamline"
