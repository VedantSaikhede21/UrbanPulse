# Problem Section — 3 Narrative Concepts

## Current State
The Problem section currently answers: *"What problem does UrbanPulse solve?"* — but as an information block, not a story. It describes "black box systems" abstractly with a pipeline diagram. It informs but doesn't move anyone.

## Goal
Replace with a narrative that answers exactly **one emotional question** per concept. The user should *feel* the problem before the solution is revealed.

---

## Concept 1: "A Tuesday Morning" — The Specific Moment

**Question it answers:** *What does a broken system feel like?*

**Approach:** Ultra-specific, cinematic. One neighborhood, one hour, one resident.

**Copy direction:**

```
It's 6:47 AM on a Tuesday.

A streetlight outside Ananya's window has been flickering for 11 days. She reported it on the municipal portal twice. The first time, she got an auto-reply. The second, nothing.

The light is still flickering.

In the next 24 hours, three more residents on her street will report the same pothole. None will know the others exist. A water leak on MG Road will get marked "resolved" without being inspected. An officer will spend 2 hours manually sorting PDF complaints.

Ananya doesn't need a dashboard. She needs the light fixed.
```

**Why it works:**
- Specific person, specific problem, specific duration ("11 days")
- Shows system failure through experience, not explanation
- Ends on emotional truth: "She needs the light fixed"
- Creates "tension" that UrbanPulse resolves

**Risk:** May feel like a fictional case study. Needs to land as *representative*, not *made up*.

---

## Concept 2: "The Invisible City" — What No One Sees

**Question it answers:** *Where do reports actually go after you press submit?*

**Approach:** Reveals the hidden machinery — and then reveals what UrbanPulse makes visible.

**Copy direction:**

```
You hit "Submit."

Your complaint enters a city no one sees. A queue of 3,000 unprioritised reports. PDFs printed and stacked. Routes without maps. The officer who covers your ward hasn't been updated in 18 months.

The system was built for paper. It still runs on paper.

UrbanPulse makes the invisible visible. Every report, triaged by AI in seconds. Every route, optimised. Every resolution, tracked — from submission to verification. Not a dashboard. A nervous system.
```

**Why it works:**
- Builds on a universal experience ("Submit" button → silence)
- The "invisible city" metaphor is memorable
- Reveal structure: here's the mess → here's how we fix it
- "A nervous system" is a fresh metaphor

**Risk:** The "invisible city" metaphor could feel vague. Needs concrete details (3000 reports, 18 months) to land.

---

## Concept 3: "One Street, Two Worlds" — Before/After Contrast

**Question it answers:** *What's the difference between today and UrbanPulse?*

**Approach:** Same street, same problem, two timelines side by side. No dystopia. Just precise, factual contrast.

**Copy direction:**

```
[Left / "Today"]
A tree branch snaps a power line on Rose Street at 3:14 PM.
A resident calls. An email is sent. A ticket is handwritten.
The ticket sits in a pile.
3 days later, the power line is still down. No one knows whose pile it's in.

[Right / "UrbanPulse"]
A tree branch snaps a power line on Rose Street at 3:14 PM.
A resident snaps a photo. AI classes it: Hazard — Power — Urgent.
The system routes it to the electrical team and traffic control simultaneously.
Estimated repair: 2.5 hours. The resident gets a notification before the crew arrives.
```

**Why it works:**
- Specific time ("3:14 PM") makes it real
- Side-by-side contrast is immediately understandable
- No abstract claims — just what happens in each world
- The "notification before the crew arrives" is a wow detail

**Risk:** Two-column layout is harder to implement. May feel like a feature comparison.

## Comparison

| Dimension | Concept 1 (Tuesday Morning) | Concept 2 (Invisible City) | Concept 3 (One Street) |
|-----------|------|------|------|
| Emotional impact | Highest | High | Medium |
| Memorability | High (specific detail) | Medium (metaphor) | High (contrast) |
| Implementation ease | Medium (copy + simple bg) | Medium (copy + visual) | Higher (two columns or toggle) |
| Risk | Feels fictional | Could be vague | Feels like comparison table |
| Uniqueness | Most distinctive | Strong metaphor | Clearest logic |

## Recommended: Concept 1
Concept 1 has the highest emotional impact and is the most distinctive from what currently exists. The key danger (feeling fictional) is mitigated by using precise, realistic detail and avoiding names that sound made up. Implement as full-width copy with minimal visual — let the words do the work.
