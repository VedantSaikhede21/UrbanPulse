# Hero v2 — Deep Research

## The Four Questions

### Why does my eye go there first?

| Site | Answer |
|------|--------|
| **Stripe** | The headline anchors the value proposition (financial infrastructure). Below it, a live product screenshot with real data acts as the gravitational center. The eye lands on the headline, then drops to the product. |
| **Linear** | The product interface screenshot dominates the entire hero. It's large, functional, and immediately tells you what the tool does. The eye goes to the issue list because it looks like real work happening. |
| **Vercel** | A code editor with a CLI deployment log in progress. The eye goes to the terminal output because it's *happening now* — the deploy is live. |
| **Framer** | The product canvas showing an AI agent in the middle of editing a hero section. The eye goes to the thinking steps ("Analyzed hero section 3s...") because it shows process. |
| **Apple** | A single product photo at massive scale. The eye has nowhere else to go. The product IS the hero. |
| **Raycast** | The keyboard visual dominates the hero. The eye goes to the keyboard because it's the largest, most distinctive element. |
| **Pitch** | A presentation slide workspace with real content. The eye goes to the slide being edited. |
| **Notion** | The product interface with AI chat overlay. The eye goes to the AI interaction because it shows the "thinking together" promise. |

**Pattern**: Every site uses a large, functional product screenshot/interface as the visual anchor. Nothing decorative. The eye goes to the *product doing work*.

### Why does this hero feel premium?

| Site | Answer |
|------|--------|
| **Stripe** | Generous whitespace. The brand color (dark navy/purple) sets a serious, financial tone. Everything feels considered — the spacing, the typography scale, the subtle gradient on the dashboard mockup. Nothing is cheap or flashy. |
| **Linear** | The interface mockup has subtle grid lines, carefully chosen typography (Inter), and enough realism that it feels like a real product, not a marketing screenshot. |
| **Apple** | Extreme visual restraint. One product, one headline, one CTA. The product photo has perfect lighting and shadow. The typography is minimal. |
| **Vercel** | Monospace font for the deploy output creates a developer-authentic feel. The black/white/cyan palette feels technical and serious. |
| **Framer** | The agent's "thinking" text and step-by-step output creates authenticity. It feels like watching someone actually use the product. |
| **Raycast** | The hardware keyboard as hero visual is unexpected and tactile. It grounds the digital product in physical reality. |
| **Pitch** | Clean, modern sans-serif. The slide editor in the background looks real, not a mockup. The gradient is subtle. |
| **Notion** | The interface looks like real Notion pages with real content. The blue AI accent suggests intelligence. |

**Pattern**: Premium comes from **restraint** (fewer elements, more space), **authenticity** (real interfaces, not mockups), and **intentionality** (every element has a purpose).

### Why do I immediately understand the product?

| Site | Answer |
|------|--------|
| **Stripe** | The headline says *exactly* what Stripe is: "Financial infrastructure to grow your revenue." The subhead and product screenshot reinforce: payments dashboard. |
| **Linear** | "The product development system" — category first. Then the screenshot shows issues, agents, and projects. 2 seconds to understand. |
| **Vercel** | "Agentic Infrastructure" + a CLI deploy log = deploy platform for AI apps. The terminal output is unmistakable. |
| **Framer** | "The AI website builder" + a canvas with a website being built. You see the prompt, the thinking, the result. |
| **Apple** | A photo of a laptop or phone. The headline says the product name. You cannot misunderstand. |
| **Raycast** | "Your shortcut to everything" + a keyboard visual. Launcher app. Instant. |
| **Pitch** | "The AI presentation workspace" + a slide editor. Presentation tool. Clear. |
| **Notion** | "The AI workspace" + a workspace interface. Docs + AI. Unmistakable. |

**Pattern**: Every site puts the **category** in the first 3-4 words of the headline. The screenshot *shows* exactly what that category looks like. No ambiguity. No cleverness that obscures.

### Why do I want to scroll?

| Site | Answer |
|------|--------|
| **Stripe** | The product screenshot is cropped — you know there's more to see below. The social proof (brand names) calls for more. |
| **Linear** | The product screenshot is cropped at the bottom. You need to scroll to see the full feature set. |
| **Vercel** | The deploy log is mid-output — you want to see what happens next. |
| **Framer** | The agent is mid-thinking. You scroll to see the result. The canvas has more below. |
| **Apple** | Multiple product rows below create a clear visual hierarchy. You scroll to see what else. |
| **Raycast** | The keyboard ends before the full set of features. You scroll for extensions, AI, automation. |
| **Pitch** | The slide is cropped. Features below. Social proof below. Clear content promise. |
| **Notion** | The interface crops naturally. Features, agents, social proof below. Clear what's next. |

**Pattern**: The hero shows enough to intrigue but not enough to satisfy. The visual is **deliberately cropped** or **shows a process in progress** so scrolling feels natural, not forced. Social proof below creates curiosity.

## Synthesis: The Universal Hero Formula

```
[Category headline]  ← first 3-4 words establish what this is
[Sub-value prop]     ← one line of specific benefit
[Product interface]  ← LARGE, REAL, FUNCTIONAL. Not a mockup. Shows the product doing work.
[Cropped bottom]     ← the interface is cropped or the process is incomplete — scroll is inevitable
[1-2 CTAs]           ← primary action + secondary (watch demo / learn)
[Optional: metric]   ← ONLY if real and specific
[Optional: social]   ← brand logos or quote, very subtle
```

## Concepts for UrbanPulse

### Concept 1 — "The Pipeline" (Recommended)

**Centerpiece**: A ticket/issue visibly flowing through 4-5 agent stages in real time. Each agent stage shows: the agent name, what it decided, and the next action. It's not just labels — it's a live ticket moving.

**Headline**: "Civic infrastructure triage. Powered by AI."
**Subhead**: "9 specialized agents route citizen reports to the right officer, in the right language, with verification — in minutes, not days."
**Visual**: A horizontal or vertical pipeline visualization. A real-looking ticket card animates through stages: Triage (Pothole detected) → Verify (Photo confirmed) → Route (Dept: Roads → Ward: 14) → Notify (Officer assigned) → Resolve (Verified).
**CTA**: "Report an Issue" / "Watch the Pipeline"

**Why it works**: 
- Shows the *product* not a *description* of the product
- The pipeline is UrbanPulse's strongest differentiator
- The ticket moving creates a natural scroll cue (process continues below)
- Category is clear immediately: civic complaint triage

### Concept 2 — "The Split" (Strong alternative)

**Centerpiece**: Two panels side by side. Left: "Before UrbanPulse" — a dark, vague interface showing a black-box complaint form with a "status: unknown" label. Right: "With UrbanPulse" — the same complaint with a visible agent pipeline showing exactly where it is.

**Headline**: "Citizens shouldn't report into a void."
**Subhead**: "UrbanPulse makes civic infrastructure visible — from complaint to resolution, every step is transparent."
**CTA**: "Try It Now" / "See How It Works"

**Why it works**:
- The problem-solution contrast is emotionally compelling
- Immediately explains WHY this product exists
- Creates curiosity about how the transparent side works
- The "before" panel is deliberately dark/mysterious

### Concept 3 — "The Dashboard" (Safe, proven)

**Centerpiece**: A realistic city dashboard showing: UHS score, live complaint count, ward map with heat colors, recent agent activity feed.

**Headline**: "Your city's health, in real time."
**Subhead**: "Monitor, triage, and resolve infrastructure issues with 9 AI agents working across every ward."
**CTA**: "Explore the Dashboard" / "Report an Issue"

**Why it works**:
- Pattern follows Stripe/Vercel/Notion exactly
- Most familiar to users
- Shows real data (UHS score, complaints)
- Risk: looks like every other dashboard

### Concept 4 — "The Citizen's Journey" (Emotional)

**Centerpiece**: A split or timeline showing the citizen's experience. Top: A citizen taking a photo of a pothole on their phone. Middle: The AI agents processing it. Bottom: A "resolved" notification on the citizen's phone. All in one visual flow.

**Headline**: "From photo to resolved. AI in between."
**Subhead**: "Snap a pothole. AI routes it to the right department. Officers fix it. Verified."
**CTA**: "Report an Issue" / "Read the Story"

**Why it works**:
- Emotional storytelling creates connection
- Shows real people benefit, not just dashboards
- The "from...to..." structure is instantly understandable
- Risk: harder to execute in pure UI — may need illustration

### Concept 5 — "The Live City" (Ambitious)

**Centerpiece**: A stylized city map/isometric view with complaint pins appearing, agents routing them (shown as glowing paths), and resolution markers lighting up. The city breathes — data flows.

**Headline**: "See what your city is telling you."
**Subhead**: "UrbanPulse listens in every language, routes to every department, and tracks every resolution."
**CTA**: "See the Live Map" / "Report an Issue"

**Why it works**:
- Most visually memorable
- Ties directly to the "city" brand
- Shows scale and real-time nature
- Risk: complex to build, performance concerns, may feel gimmicky if not polished

---

## Recommendation

**Start with Concept 1 (The Pipeline)** for Hero v2. It's the most distinctive, most product-authentic, and most aligned with UrbanPulse's core differentiator. The linear flow (complaint → agents → resolution) is inherently understandable and emotionally satisfying.

**Hold Concept 2 (The Split)** as a strong alternative if the pipeline doesn't feel right after prototyping.

**Defer Concept 5 (Live City)** to a later sprint — it's ambitious and would benefit from data infrastructure improvements first.

## Rejected Approaches

| Approach | Why |
|----------|-----|
| Abstract gradients with text | ANTI_PATTERNS.md: nothing decorative. Every premium site shows product. |
| Animated 3D city | Performance cost, reduced-motion complexity, risks feeling gimmicky. |
| Video background | Slow to load, distracts from message, accessibility issues. |
| Carousel/multiple hero messages | ANTI_PATTERNS.md. World-class sites never use carousels as heroes. |
| Hero v1 with more polish | Creative Director advised against. The concept itself needs to change. |
